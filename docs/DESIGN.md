# Lunowa Marketing Visual Contract

Status: canonical visual direction for the initial marketing site, 2026-08-29.

## 1. Design thesis

The site should feel like **Calm Product Cinema**:

- familiar enough to understand instantly;
- quiet enough to preserve attention;
- distinctive through Product behavior rather than decorative novelty;
- premium through precision, restraint, hierarchy, and real Product proof.

The page is not a generic AI landing page and not an art experiment.

## 2. Visual priorities

In descending order:

1. comprehension;
2. Product proof;
3. trust;
4. action clarity;
5. brand distinctiveness;
6. delight.

Decoration must never outrank comprehension or proof.

## 3. Layout

Use a familiar high-trust marketing structure:

`Header -> Hero -> Product proof -> Problem -> Distinction -> Product system -> Trust -> Availability/FAQ -> CTA/Footer`.

Prefer full-width narrative bands with constrained inner content. Cards are for genuine repeated items, framed Product surfaces, modals, or compact comparisons — not as default section containers.

Avoid card-inside-card composition.

## 4. Hero

- centered primary copy;
- clear H1 and one dominant CTA;
- secondary `仕組みを見る` action;
- one large Product stage immediately below;
- Product/brand must be a first-viewport signal;
- hint of the next section should remain visible where practical.

Do not use a generic split hero with marketing copy on one side and a decorative card on the other unless real visual testing proves it materially clearer.

## 5. Product visuals

The primary visual asset is believable Lunowa UI, not stock photography or abstract AI art.

The Product stage must show semantic states such as:

- Managed;
- intermediate evidence update;
- Still Managed;
- Needs You;
- Moment;
- Source/provenance.

Reuse one coherent scenario so visitors perceive one system rather than a feature gallery.

## 6. Color direction

Target character:

- warm or neutral near-white background;
- near-black/deep-neutral primary text;
- restrained indigo/cobalt accent;
- calm blue for Managed semantics;
- restrained amber for Needs You semantics;
- red reserved for genuine integrity/error states.

Avoid one-note purple-blue AI palettes, dominant gradients, neon, excessive beige/cream branding, or decorative color blobs.

All semantic states require text/icon redundancy; color alone is not meaning.

## 7. Shape / depth

- restrained radius; avoid universal pillification;
- subtle borders;
- very light shadows only where spatial hierarchy needs them;
- avoid glassmorphism as a system-level style;
- avoid floating sections that look like disconnected dashboard cards.

## 8. Typography

- prioritize Japanese readability and scanning;
- large type only for true Hero/section statements;
- compact Product UI uses correspondingly tighter type;
- no negative letter-spacing tricks that harm Japanese or localization;
- layout must survive longer English/Spanish strings and browser zoom/text scaling.

## 9. Imagery / illustration

- Product UI is primary proof;
- generated bitmap illustration may support brand campaigns/OGP/secondary atmosphere;
- do not use generic stock-person photography merely to make the site look populated;
- no AI orb, bokeh blob, abstract brain, circuit-network cliché, or decorative 3D scene unless a later evidence-backed design task proves clear value.

## 10. Information density

The page should not feel sparse for its own sake. It should feel selective.

Each viewport should have a clear focal object. Supporting content recedes.

Do not give every text block, icon, border, and motion equal visual weight.

## 11. Responsive visual behavior

Mobile is not a scaled-down desktop.

- preserve the same narrative order;
- simplify the Product stage rather than shrinking it illegibly;
- reduce simultaneous content;
- keep CTA and Product proof visible early;
- preserve semantic state distinctions;
- no horizontal overflow at 320 CSS px;
- target controls should be comfortably touchable.

## 12. Anti-patterns

Reject by default:

- scroll-jacking;
- giant decorative 3D hero;
- autoplay cinematic video as the primary proof;
- endlessly moving logo marquees;
- typewriter effects for ordinary copy;
- decorative cursor followers;
- excessive parallax;
- permanent background particles;
- sections presented as dozens of rounded feature cards;
- visuals that imply functionality the Product does not have;
- invented screenshots detached from the canonical Product semantics.

## 13. Quality bar

The desired impression is not `AI-generated website`.

It should read as intentionally designed software marketing: calm, exact, credible, and visually coherent with the Product's promise that attention is only requested when necessary.
